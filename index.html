CogniCare: caregiver intelligence

AI Based Web Application for Vascular Dementia Caregiver Support

CogniCare helps caregivers and clinicians who support people with vascular dementia. Track brain health from MRI, speech and cognitive tests, monitor stress, plan safe meals, and detect falls during home rehabilitation, all in one web platform.

The CogniCare platform

Six care tools, one architecture, measurable results.

Six care tools

Our unified platform runs advanced AI pipelines that support vascular dementia caregivers with continuous, personalized monitoring. Pick a tool to see what it does.

Choose a care tool

Cognitive assessment and Brain Health Index

Combines 2D structural T1-weighted MRI morphometrics (EfficientNet-B0), acoustic speech DSP and a dynamic randomized MMSE into one Brain Health Index (BHI).

In plain terms: one score built from a brain scan, a voice sample and a short memory test.

  • EfficientNet-B0
  • MRI morphometrics
  • Speech DSP
  • MMSE

Atrophy alerting

Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) statistical process control automatically alerts caregivers to sub-acute atrophy.

In plain terms: it smooths out the noise in each new result and warns when the trend slowly slips.

  • EWMA
  • Statistical process control

Facial stress monitor

Continuous facial expression tracking with a PyTorch EmotionCNN, mapped into operational stress levels.

In plain terms: the webcam reads expressions and turns them into a simple calm, moderate or high level.

  • PyTorch
  • EmotionCNN
  • Facial expression analysis

Distress forecasting

A Random Forest model predicts near-term (30-minute) behavioral distress and gives real-time de-escalation guidance.

In plain terms: a heads-up before a difficult moment, with ideas for what to try.

  • Random Forest
  • 30-minute forecast

Smart nutrition engine

Multi-target CatBoost classification of 11 health features generates personalized meal plans and texture modifications, with deterministic safety gates that route renal and dysphagia risks to specialists.

In plain terms: meals that match how a person can safely eat, and a referral when a risk needs an expert.

  • CatBoost
  • Multi-target classification
  • Clinical safety gates

Edge rehab and fall detection

On-device 33-landmark pose estimation (MediaPipe BlazePose, WASM) monitors physical therapy without hardware sensors, with finite-state machine fall detection and an 880 Hz Web Audio siren.

In plain terms: the camera on the device follows body movement, and an alarm sounds if a fall happens.

  • MediaPipe BlazePose
  • WASM edge inference
  • Finite-state machine
  • Web Audio alerts

How the platform fits together

A decoupled architecture connects the browser to scalable AI services.

In the browser

React 18 web client

Browser UI, webcam capture and on-device MediaPipe BlazePose (WASM) inference.

In the middle

FastAPI server

Asynchronous API layer that coordinates all four modules, with MongoDB for data storage.

Behind the scenes

AI inference pipelines

EfficientNet-B0, EmotionCNN, Random Forest, CatBoost and EWMA alerting.

Research domain

The problem we set out to solve, how we approached it, and what we used.

Literature survey

We reviewed existing work in six areas. In each, a strong tool exists, but none of them runs continuously at home for the caregiver.

Vascular dementia and its burden

O'Brien and Thomas (2015) describe vascular dementia as a condition that often declines in steps rather than smoothly, and the VASCOG statement (Sachdev et al., 2014) sets out diagnostic criteria for vascular cognitive disorders. The World Alzheimer Report (Prince et al., 2015) and the WHO Global Action Plan (2017) show how large the global burden is and how much of it falls on families. The Zarit burden interview (Zarit et al., 1980) is the classic way to measure the strain on caregivers.

Cognitive assessment

The Mini-Mental State Examination (Folstein et al., 1975) is still a standard bedside screen. It is quick, but it is a fixed set of questions given at occasional clinic visits, so it cannot show gradual change between visits.

Brain imaging and deep learning

Voxel-based morphometry (Ashburner and Friston, 2000) compares brain volume across regions, and the OASIS dataset (Marcus et al., 2007) provides MRI scans of both healthy and demented older adults. Deep learning (LeCun et al., 2015) and EfficientNet (Tan and Le, 2019) make accurate image models practical on modest hardware, which supports automated atrophy screening.

Stress and facial expression

Ekman and Friesen (1971) showed that basic facial expressions are recognised across cultures, and the FER-2013 contest report (Goodfellow et al., 2013) gave the field a standard benchmark. Random forests (Breiman, 2001) are a robust choice for forecasting from tabular signals.

Nutrition and dysphagia

Many people with vascular dementia have trouble swallowing. The IDDSI framework (Cichero et al., 2017) defines safe food textures and drink thickness. CatBoost (Prokhorenkova et al., 2018) handles categorical health data well, which suits meal classification.

Fall detection and monitoring

Igual et al. (2013) review the challenges of fall detection, such as false alarms and the need for wearable devices. BlazePose (Bazarevsky et al., 2020) and MediaPipe (Lugaresi et al., 2019) now allow real-time pose tracking in an ordinary browser with no extra hardware. Roberts (1959) introduced the exponentially weighted moving average chart, which is a simple way to spot a slow drift in a score over time.

Research problem

Vascular dementia progresses in a stepwise, unpredictable trajectory. Episodic hospital visits fail to capture sub-acute atrophy, home rehabilitation is prone to fall hazards, and generic nutrition tools ignore dysphagia risks.

Research gap

There is a gap between episodic clinical reviews and continuous home telemetry. CogniCare bridges it with monitoring that runs every day at home.

Objectives

Main objective: build one web platform that gives vascular dementia caregivers continuous, personalized monitoring.

  • Combine MRI, speech and a randomized MMSE into a Brain Health Index with atrophy alerts.
  • Track stress from facial expressions and forecast distress 30 minutes ahead.
  • Generate dysphagia-safe meal plans and send renal and swallowing risks to specialists.
  • Monitor home rehabilitation and detect falls on the device, without extra sensors.

Methodology

Cognitive assessment

EfficientNet-B0 on T1-weighted MRI, speech DSP and a randomized MMSE, merged into the BHI and watched with EWMA.

Stress management

EmotionCNN reads facial expressions; a Random Forest forecasts distress.

Nutrition

CatBoost classifies 11 health features, and safety gates route risks to specialists.

Edge rehabilitation

BlazePose in the browser, with a finite-state machine for falls and an audio alert.
